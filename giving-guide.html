PRACTICAL FREE-GIVEAWAY ADVICE

How to give away unwanted items responsibly

Giving away something useful keeps it out of storage or landfill and helps somebody nearby. These steps make the handover simpler for everyone.

1. Decide whether the item is genuinely usable

Before listing an item, check whether it works, whether any pieces are missing, and whether it is clean enough for someone else to use. A broken item might still suit a person seeking spare parts, but describe it as broken rather than implying it works. Don't offer recalled or unsafe products.

2. Take clear, current photos

Photograph the actual item from more than one angle, in good light. Show scratches, stains, dents or other damage instead of hiding them. For furniture, a photo from the front and side is often helpful; for electronics, include the model information but avoid exposing serial numbers or personal data.

3. Write a description that prevents wasted trips

Include measurements for large furniture, approximate age if known, condition, whether an item needs to be dismantled, and whether someone needs a car or trailer to collect it. Give your suburb rather than a precise home address in a public listing. For appliances, state honestly whether you have tested them recently.

4. Remove personal information

Sign out of accounts and factory-reset phones, tablets, computers and smart-home devices before handing them over. Remove SIM cards and memory cards. Check drawers, handbags, books and folders for documents with names, addresses or financial details.

5. Arrange collection without payment

Zero Dollar Store listings are $0: there is no price, deposit, handling fee or paid delivery. Confirm an agreed day and time with the recipient. Where practical, arrange a public or otherwise safe handover and avoid posting your precise address publicly. If somebody asks you to pay to secure a free item, stop the exchange and report the concern.

6. Handle large and sensitive items with care

For bulky furniture, be clear about stairs, access and who will lift the item. Check current local rules before giving away or disposing of mattresses, child car restraints, electrical goods, batteries or other safety-sensitive items. Never assume an old item is safe merely because it looks intact.

7. Close the loop

Once an item has been collected, mark it as unavailable or remove the listing so other people don't keep enquiring. If the recipient does not show up, contact the next interested person rather than promising the same item to multiple people at once.

Not everything should be given away. If an item is hazardous, recalled or beyond safe repair, use an appropriate local disposal or recycling service instead of passing the risk to someone else.
